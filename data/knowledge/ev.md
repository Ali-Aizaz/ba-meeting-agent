# EV

An EV (battery electric vehicle, or BEV) is an electric vehicle driven by one or more electric motors. The energy is stored in a high-voltage traction battery pack (measured in kWh) that is charged from an external supply. An inverter converts DC battery power to AC for the traction motor, and a battery management system (BMS) monitors cell voltage, temperature, and state of charge. There is no fuel tank and no exhaust pipe.

## Charging

Home and workplace charging (Level 1 and Level 2) uses an AC supply and the vehicle's onboard charger, which is slower. A public DC fast charger bypasses the onboard charger to deliver DC power directly to the battery pack and is much quicker.

## Battery chemistry

Modern EV battery packs commonly use lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells for lower cost, thermal stability, and long cycle life, or nickel manganese cobalt (NMC) cells for higher energy density and cold-weather range.
