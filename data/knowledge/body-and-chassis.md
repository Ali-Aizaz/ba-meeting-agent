# Body and chassis

The body is the outer structure and cabin of the car. The chassis is the structural frame and underbody that carries the suspension, steering, wheels, brakes, and powertrain. A unibody (monocoque) design joins the body and the frame into one lighter, stiffer structure used on most passenger cars and crossovers. A body-on-frame design mounts a separate body on a rigid ladder frame, common on heavy-duty trucks and off-road SUVs.

## Suspension

The suspension (springs, dampers/shock absorbers, and control arms such as MacPherson strut or multi-link) keeps the tyres in contact with the road, controls body roll, and isolates the cabin from bumps.

## Drivetrain and brakes

Drivetrain layouts include front-wheel drive (FWD), rear-wheel drive (RWD), and all-wheel drive (AWD / 4WD). Hydraulic disc brakes use calipers and rotors to slow the wheels, working alongside regenerative braking on hybrids and EVs.
